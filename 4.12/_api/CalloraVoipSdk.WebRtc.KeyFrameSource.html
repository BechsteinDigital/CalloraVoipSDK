Where IsKeyFrame came from. Two sources answer that question, and they do not survive end-to-end encryption equally well — a forwarder that needs to trust the flag needs to know which one it got.

public enum KeyFrameSource

Fields

Payload = 2

Derived from the payload by the depacketiser (the VP8 P bit, an H.264 IDR NAL unit). Authoritative for a session in the clear. For a sender that encrypts its frames, it holds only as far as that sender leaves the relevant bytes readable — H.264's NAL headers are left in the clear by every shipping implementation, VP8's key-frame bit is not guaranteed to be.

RtpHeaderExtension = 1

The sender's Dependency Descriptor, an RTP header extension written before any payload encryption. Trustworthy whatever the payload holds — this is the source to require when frames may be encrypted end to end (RFC 9605).

Unknown = 0

No key-frame signal was available. The frame's IsKeyFrame is false and means unknown, not no: this is what an opaque video session (OpaqueVideoFrames) reports when the peer negotiated no Dependency Descriptor, and what every audio frame reports.

Remarks

Only relevant to video: audio frames are never key frames and always report Unknown.
